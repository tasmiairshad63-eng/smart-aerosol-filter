Catch. Clean. Sense. Warn.

A research journey toward smart aerosol nanofiber filters

From passive aerosol capture to self-sanitizing and self-warning filtration systems.

The Big Problem

Most aerosol filters only capture particles. But captured aerosols may still contain harmful microbes, toxic gases, or pollutants. The filter usually cannot clean itself or warn the user when it becomes unsafe.

So the real question is: can a filter become intelligent?

Paper 1 — Capture

Zhang et al. 2018

This paper explains how aerosols behave when they land on nanofibers. It is the foundation paper because before designing better filters, we must first understand how particles are captured.

Main role:
Understanding aerosol capture
Method:
In-situ nanoscale observation
Gap:
Passive capture only

Paper 2 — Clean

Li et al. 2021

This paper moves from passive capture to active protection by developing a self-sanitizing reusable mask. The idea is that captured harmful aerosols should not remain dangerous.

Main role:
Self-sanitizing filter
Method:
Electrospun nanofibers + photocatalysis
Gap:
No real-time sensing or warning

Paper 3 — Sense

Lee et al. 2023

This paper adds intelligence by creating a nanofiber filter that can remove particles and gases, while also giving a visible color warning for ammonia exposure.

Main role:
Smart multifunctional filter
Method:
MOF-functionalized nanofiber membrane
Gap:
No self-sanitization or full biodegradability

The Research Gap

The three papers solve different parts of the same problem. One explains capture, one adds self-cleaning, and one adds sensing. But no single practical system combines all functions together.

Aerosol capture
Self-sanitization
Gas removal
Visible warning
Biodegradability

Our Proposed Idea

A biodegradable smart nanofiber aerosol filter that can catch, clean, sense, and warn.

It would capture PM2.5 and bioaerosols, self-sanitize under light, remove harmful gases, and change color when the filter becomes unsafe or saturated.

Final Message

The future aerosol filter should not be silent. It should actively protect the user by combining nanofiber capture, photocatalytic self-cleaning, MOF-based gas removal, and visible sensing.

From passive protection to intelligent aerosol safety.
